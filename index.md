Prigogine's work on dissipative structures and far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics actually opens up some interesting possibilities for reconciliation with teleology, though it depends on what kind of teleology you have in mind.

Prigogine showed that complex systems can spontaneously self-organize and increase in complexity when they're far from thermodynamic equilibrium. This creates a kind of directionality in nature—systems evolving toward more complex, organized states under the right conditions. This isn't teleology in the classical Aristotelian sense of intrinsic purposes or final causes, but it does suggest that the universe has inherent tendencies toward complexity and organization rather than being purely random or mechanical.

The reconciliation becomes more plausible if you're thinking about "teleonomy" rather than strong teleology—goal-directedness that emerges from physical processes rather than being imposed externally or existing as a metaphysical feature. Prigogine's dissipative structures show how order can arise from chaos through purely physical processes, which could be seen as a naturalized form of directedness.

Where it gets tricky is if you want classical teleology with consciousness, intention, or cosmic purpose built in from the start. Prigogine was explicitly trying to give a physical account of emergence and creativity in nature without invoking metaphysical additions. But some philosophers and complexity theorists have argued that his work at least makes the universe more hospitable to teleological thinking than strict mechanistic determinism did.

What kind of teleological framework are you thinking about? That would help clarify whether there's genuine compatibility or just superficial resonance.
